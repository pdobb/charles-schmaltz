ObituaryCharles Raymond Schmaltz · October 14, 1951 – November 1, 2026

Charles Raymond Schmaltz, age 75, passed away on November 1st, 2026 at home in Robert’s Cove, LA.

Charles was born on October 14, 1951 in Crowley, LA to Leonard Joseph Schmalz and Fannie Moore Schmalz. The youngest of three children, Charles grew up in South Crowley, looking to his older brother, Bill, as his hero. Charles graduated from Crowley High School in 1969 and attended Louisiana Technical College where he was certified in automotive paint and body. Charles was exceptionally gifted in his field and was quickly offered employment at the well-respected paint and body shop of Nefor Bertrand. It was here that Charles coupled his skills and talent with a developing love of antique car restoration, a passion he would carry throughout his life.

With the ambition of seeking stable income and the desire to start a family of his own, Charles sought full time employment with the Crowley Fire Department (CFD) in 1973. In addition to his full-time responsibilities at CFD, Charles continued paint and body work at the special request of Mr. Bertrand. Over time, Charles transitioned toward building what would be a 33-year career at CFD where he thrived within the community and brotherhood of the firefighters. Charles rose through the ranks of the fire department, eventually receiving a promotion to Captain and then achieving the commendation of Fire Fighter of the Year in 1978. All the while, he established Charlie’s Hobby Shop, where he tackled minor automotive repairs and nurtured his love of restoring antique cars. In 1972, he enlisted in the Army National Guard in Co C in 156th Infantry in Jennings, LA. After six years of service, Charles separated with an honorable discharge at the rank of Sergeant E5. Charles continued his work as a full-time firefighter until 2006 when he retired from civil service as a member of the International Association of Firefighters Local 1442. Upon retiring, Charles immediately transitioned to full time employment in the Maintenance Department of the Acadia Parish School Board. Through this work, he earned a second retirement in 2022. Charles’ family reflects upon his incredible work ethic and driven nature, recalling that, for most of his life, Charles worked multiple jobs for love and support of his wife and children.Charles married Helen Lorraine Neumeyer (Crowley), truly the love of his life, in December of 1973. The couple enjoyed 53 wonderful years of marriage together, raising a family of three children: Chuck Schmaltz (wife Heidi) of Mire, Gretchen DobbinSchmaltz (husband Paul) of The Woodlands, TX, and Liv-Claire Schmaltz of Lake Charles.

To Helen and his children, Charles was known as “Bear,” an affectionate nickname taken from the children’s story Goldilocks and the Three Bears. Charles relished his family and always put them first. When his children were very young, he and Helen moved their family to the country in Robert’s Cove. As he deeply valued his childhood relationship with animals and nature, Charles wanted to provide such an experience for his own family. Together the family raised calves, hatched chickens and ducks, and witnessed the birth of piglets, horses, and kittens. They enjoyed one-on-one time with their Dad in the form of early morning horse-back rides, pitching lessons, and working on special projects together. He taught Chuck to weld at the age of eight years old, and together, they designed and built the shop that still stands on their farm today. Bear enthusiastically assisted with school projects, from helping Gretchen to construct a donkey head for a class play (from a baseball helmet and chicken wire, of course!), to attending Liv’s class field trips and performances. As the children grew, he and Helen followed them all over the country to watch baseball and softball games, attend graduations from colleges and military programs, and even to deliver 10 lbs of crawfish étouffée to Liv’s Naval posting at Fort Huachuca, AZ. Charles poured himself, heart and soul, into raising his family.

Charles had a particular appreciation for history, and a fondness for reviving the “old ways” of doing things. Often, he jokingly commented that he might have been born in the wrong era. He took great pride in his penmanship, learned to turn soil with a horse-drawn antique plow, raised livestock from which to case homemade sausage, and the list goes on. Though a reluctant flier, Charles always considered his trip to Rome one of his greatest adventures and spoke often of his amazement at the ancient ruins and their innovative construction.

Charles truly devoted his life to his family. He loved Helen dearly, and was so incredibly proud of their children. He generously shared his time, gifts, and talents for the benefit of his children and his community. Through his example, he instilled in his family the value of hard work and the virtue of selflessness. He leaves behind a family and a community who will miss him deeply, and honor the memory of such a special life - well-lived and treasured.

In addition to his wife and children, Charles is survived by his grandchildren: Norma Dedear (Lake Charles), Gabe Schmaltz (Lafayette), Madison (husband Jontrey) Quebodeaux (Cankton), and Kaleb Cormier (Mire); and great-grandchild Johnnie Mae Quebodeaux (Cankton). He is also survived by his brother William “Bill” Schmaltz (wife Roberta) (Crowley).

Charles is preceded in death by his parents Leonard J. and Fannie Schmalz, and his sister Victoria Schmalz.

Pallbearers will be Gabe Schmaltz, Paul DobbinSchmaltz, Jason Schmaltz, Willie Neumeyer, Ben Bertrand, and Ronald Pippin. Arrangements have been entrusted to Geesey-Ferguson Funeral Home in Crowley.
